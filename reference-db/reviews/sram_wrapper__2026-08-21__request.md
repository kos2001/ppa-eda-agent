Physical-constraint-evaluator independent read: sram_wrapper RSZ-0090 (hd-library path)

Scope check first. My checklist is density / legalization / congestion. Strictly, none of those three actually apply to this failure. RSZ-0090 fires inside repair_design's max_transition DRV check on the macro's own input pins — it's a liberty-driven transition-time violation, not a density overflow, a legalization-violation count, or a global-routing congestion estimate. There is no legalization or congestion artifact this failure would even produce. So from my specific lane, items 2 and 3 of the checklist are moot for this error; only item 1 (real tool error, quoted) is in play, and the diagnosis already quotes it correctly. That's worth stating explicitly rather than silently — it confirms this genuinely isn't a "prune for density/congestion reasons" case, it's a liberty-floor case, which is consistent with feedback-optimizer's framing.

Where I did push on the numbers. The diagnosis leans on "0.01pF is essentially just the bus's own pin capacitance (0.00689pF), i.e. no additional wire" without explaining the ~45% gap between those two figures. That gap is large enough that if it represented real residual wire/net capacitance, there'd be genuine headroom — reducing load from 0.01pF to 0.00689pF (a ~31% cut) against a driver whose transition scales roughly with load could plausibly pull 0.043ns down under the 0.04ns limit, which would undercut the "physically floored" conclusion and reopen a placement-adjacency argument. I checked this rather than taking "essentially" at face value: OpenSTA/RSZ's DRV reports print capacitance loads rounded to 2 decimal places in pF, and 0.00689 rounds to 0.01 at that precision. So the 0.01pF is very plausibly just a rounded display of the same 0.00689pF bare pin cap, not a distinct larger number — i.e. no real slack is hiding in that gap. This is a stronger basis for the "no additional wire" claim than the original phrasing gave, and it holds up.

One residual gap I can't close, and won't paper over. The diagnosis's adjacency claim is inferred entirely from capacitance arithmetic, never from an actual placed net length — there's no .odb/placement report on disk to check whether the counter/subtractor really did land pin-adjacent to addr0/addr1/wmask0, or whether a macro halo/keepout (a genuine density-adjacent physical constraint) forced some minimum standoff distance that isn't zero. I can't verify this either way without run artifacts, and none exist. So it's not something I can act on now, but it's the one place a future run (if ever re-kept with .odb intact) should check directly — actual wire length, not inferred cap headroom — before fully closing this out.

Verdict: Concur with feedback-optimizer — prune, no placement-strategist candidate justified right now. The numeric near-equality survives scrutiny (rounding, not coincidence), and none of my density/legalization/congestion signals are even implicated in this failure mode. The only open item is an unverified assumption (inferred adjacency vs. measured adjacency) that isn't actionable without artifacts that don't currently exist — worth a one-line note in the case file for future runs, not a blocker to closing this session's diagnosis.
