# Reading Synopsys PrimePower / PrimeTime PX `report_power`

## Where it comes from

Generated by PrimePower (PPower, formerly PrimeTime PX) via `report_power`,
run on a netlist with switching activity annotated from a VCD or SAIF file
captured from RTL/gate-level simulation. **Power numbers are only as good as
the activity file** — a testbench that doesn't exercise realistic workload
patterns (e.g. never toggling a clock-gate enable, or running only reset/
idle sequences) will systematically under- or over-report power. Always
check what workload generated the VCD/SAIF before trusting the numbers.

## Typical structure

```
****************************************
Report : power
        -analysis_effort low
Design : top_module
Version: T-2022.03-SP5
Date   : ...
****************************************

Global Operating Voltage = 0.9
Power-specific unit information :
    Voltage Units = 1V
    Capacitance Units = 1.000000pf
    Time Units = 1ns
    Dynamic Power Units = 1mW
    Leakage Power Units = 1nW

  Cell Internal Power  =   4.2103 mW   (42.1%)
  Net Switching Power  =   3.8871 mW   (38.9%)
                         -----------
  Total Dynamic Power  =   8.0974 mW  (98.7%)

  Cell Leakage Power   = 104.3200 uW   (1.3%)
                         -----------
  Total Power          =   8.2017 mW  (100%)
```

Often followed by a hierarchical breakdown (`report_power -hierarchy`)
showing per-block contributions, and/or a `report_clock_gating` section if
clock gating is enabled.

## Key metrics and where they live

- **Cell Internal Power** — power dissipated inside cells (short-circuit
  current + internal capacitance switching) as they toggle. Scales with
  switching activity and cell drive strength.
- **Net Switching Power** — power to charge/discharge net (wire +
  downstream input pin) capacitance. Scales with net capacitance × toggle
  rate × VDD². High-fanout or long nets dominate here.
- **Total Dynamic Power** = internal + switching power. This is the
  activity-dependent, workload-dependent component.
- **Cell Leakage Power** — static power drawn regardless of switching,
  dominated by transistor sub-threshold leakage. Depends on process node,
  Vt selection, and temperature/voltage corner — *not* on the VCD/SAIF
  activity at all. If leakage is a small percentage at typical corner but
  the design also gets signed off at a hot/high-voltage corner, re-check
  leakage there — it can dominate at worst-case corners even when dynamic
  dominates at typical.
- **Percentages** — quick triage: if dynamic power dominates, focus on
  activity reduction (clock gating, operand isolation) and cell/net
  capacitance; if leakage dominates, focus on Vt mix and power gating.

## Common patterns and what they mean

- **Net switching power is unexpectedly high relative to internal power**:
  look for high-fanout nets or nets with large downstream capacitance
  (long routes, many loads) — often the same nets flagged for buf/inv
  insertion in `report-area.md` and for timing pressure in
  `report-timing.md`; the three reports frequently point at the same
  physical culprit from three angles.
- **A block's power looks high but its logic is simple**: check for
  missing or ineffective clock gating — a register bank toggling every
  cycle even when its output is unused wastes both internal and switching
  power for no functional benefit. `report_clock_gating` (if run) shows
  clock-gating efficiency per register group.
- **Leakage power is a surprisingly large fraction of total**: check the Vt
  mix — if most cells are low-Vt (fast, high-leakage) rather than only the
  cells on critical timing paths, that's a synthesis effort/library setting
  issue (DC's `set_multi_vth_constraint` / Vt-aware optimization not
  applied aggressively enough), not an inherent design cost.
- **Power numbers change drastically between two VCD-based runs of the
  "same" design**: check the activity file's simulated workload first —
  this is usually a testbench/stimulus difference, not a netlist change.

## Relationship to the other PPA legs

- **Power ↔ Timing**: clock gating trades a small amount of extra logic
  (gating cell + enable logic, minor area/timing cost) for potentially large
  dynamic power savings — usually a good trade when idle cycles are common.
  Voltage/frequency scaling (if in scope) directly trades performance
  (max frequency) for power (∝ V²f) — not visible in a single `report_power`
  run; requires re-running at each voltage/frequency corner of interest.
- **Power ↔ Area**: multi-Vt optimization can *simultaneously* reduce
  leakage power and area on non-critical paths by moving cells to a
  smaller, higher-Vt library variant — check `report-area.md`'s macro/cell
  area breakdown alongside this report when evaluating a Vt-remap pass.
